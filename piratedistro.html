Pirate Distro MA Thesis, Royal College of Art

Galileo, a condemned heretic, wrote his last books under house arrest. Rock n’ roll, censored until the late ‘60s, was broadcast from abandoned ships and disused naval forts. Until the world is ready to adopt our ideas, we circulate and steward them ourselves.

During my postgraduate study at the Royal College of Art, I challenged myself to empower this practice. Pirate Distro is the outcome. An unruly multi-modal publication, Pirate Distro provokes us to disrupt entrenched routes of expression and exchange.

I developed and applied an anarchic visual system, mismatched with intention rather than coordinated by default. Pirate Distro saw me practice a wide range of skills, from type design to metal fabrication: all work is my own unless otherwise specified.

This project is open-access: texts are free to read and share at Venn Diagramm.

Pirate Distro Reader: bootlegged excerpts, pulled from a broad body of research, establish a narrative arc for the project. Content is scanned rather than re-typeset, to preserve its original context and pay homage to the xerography movement. You can download the full publication here.
A Pirate Printer's Guide: a swashbuckler's manual for do-it-yourself print production. 48 pages + covers and sleeves; words, illustration, and risograph printing my own. This guide is free to read and share online. If you'd like to buy or sell hard copies, feel free to reach out.
Bootleg this Book(mark): a provocative reminder to redistribute knowledge where you can. Scattered shifts in glyph orientation and weight illuminate the process of letterpress printing and create a sense of scrappiness and urgency. Typeset with help from Ian Gabb.
Pirate Library: A prototype for a grassroots filesharing network. A miniature computer hosts a small, secure server: anyone in the vicinity can connect to an offline hotspot to download files they'd like to keep and upload files they'd like to share.
The prototype lives in a solander box, printed with a custom wordmark and designed to discreetly accommodate its plugins and power supplies. An accompanying leaflet, ornamented with ASCII, inducts viewers.
The server itself adapts Filebrowser, an open-source project on GitHub. I reskinned the front end, pulling from early 2000s UI to evoke the cyber-utopian, grassroots internet that defined my first years online; and creating a custom animated logomark inspired by Blackbeard's flag. Dylan Leggio kindly contributed programming and setup on the back end.
Display System 02: One of a series of aluminum displays I designed and fabricated to more flexibly scaffold printed matter. It can lay flat, lean, or wall-mount, and its shelves can be moved and removed to reapportion space.
